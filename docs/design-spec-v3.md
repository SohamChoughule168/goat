# Imaginars Club Services — Design & Build Spec v3
## "Professional at scale" — product-professional register, dual-mode

**Status:** authoritative. Supersedes `design-brief.md` (v1 dark) and `design-brief-v2.md` (v2 chromatic editorial).
**Branch:** `v3-professional`. Production (`imaginarsclubservices.com`) is not touched by any phase in this document.
**Date:** 2026-08-22.

---

## 1. Why the previous builds failed, and what that tells us

Three builds have now been rejected. Build 1 ("Forge Spectrum") was maximalist — GSAP, Lenis, WebGL, kinetic type, custom cursor, magnetic buttons, marquees. Builds 2 and 3 then failed at opposite ends of the same axis.

Build 2 was dark and restrained. It failed because subtraction over thin content reads as unfinished, not expensive. Build 3 was chromatic and loud. It failed because saturated full-bleed colour fields are the visual language of a creative agency, and an agency look is the opposite of the goal — the stated ambition is to compete with large companies, and large companies do not look like Koto case studies.

The invariant across all three is more important than any one of them: **every build so far has been chrome.** Each was a homepage with eight or so sections and a thin set of inner pages. No amount of art direction fixes that, because what makes a large company's website feel large is not its homepage. It is surface area and specificity — eighteen service pages that each answer real buyer questions, case studies with numbers, documented process, published pricing posture, an insights library, a careers page, legal pages, a security and confidentiality statement, a mega-menu that shows the full range of capability, and a tool that does something useful before anyone talks to sales.

So v3 rebalances effort: **roughly 60% of the work is content architecture and features, 40% is visual.** The register below is deliberately quieter than v2, because the depth is now doing the work that colour was being asked to do.

### The content audit, answered honestly

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Real case studies with detail? | Several clients, real engagements |
| Real photography / product imagery? | Yes, usable visuals exist |
| Real copy and value proposition? | Partial — positioning still unanswered (see §12) |
| Proprietary data, tool, or interactive asset? | Not yet — v3 builds one (the Capability Graph and the Scope Estimator) |
| Real testimonials with names, six-plus logos? | Yes, logos and testimonials available |

**Four of five yes. This is a content-rich site.** That verdict changes the strategy: restraint is now viable, because there is real material for restraint to frame. This is precisely the condition that was absent in build 2, and it is why the same register can succeed now when it failed then. Content becomes the design; chrome gets quieter; imagery runs large.

## 2. Register: product-professional, dual-mode

**Reference set:** Stripe, Linear, Vercel, Mercury, Ramp. Not Koto, not DixonBaxi, not Instrument.

**Defence against the other three registers.** Corporate authority (navy + brass + serif) would read as a law firm or a legacy consultancy — credible but slow, and wrong for a studio whose tagline is "Imagination, engineered." Tamed chromatic keeps the agency signal that just got rejected. Technical dark-first is the closest fit and is the stated personal taste, but shipping dark-only is what produced the "basic" verdict, and a dual-mode build gets that aesthetic as an option rather than an imposition. Product-professional is the only register of the four that reads as *a company* rather than *a studio*, which is the actual brief.

**One anchor hue.** Indigo, OKLCH hue 265. Everything chromatic on the site derives from it. Neutrals are tinted 265 at chroma 0.003–0.016, never pure grey — pure grey beside a saturated hue looks dead.

**Radius language:** moderate. 4 / 6 / 10 / 14 / 20 / full. Applied consistently; nested inner radius = outer − padding. (v2 used radius 0; sharp corners are part of the editorial register being retired.)

**Elevation:** light mode uses layered shadows, never a single blur. Dark mode has shadows effectively off and uses lightness elevation plus a 1px inner top highlight, because shadows are close to invisible on dark surfaces.

## 3. Tokens

Generated by `palette.py`, emitted to `tokens.css`. **Do not hand-edit the CSS** — edit the ramps in the Python and re-run, so the contrast verification and the stylesheet can never disagree.

Three tiers, and the middle tier is not skippable. Tier 1 primitives (`--neutral-*`, `--brand-*`) are never referenced by a component. Components reference tier 2 semantic tokens only (`--color-text-muted`, not `--neutral-600`). Theming is remapping tier 2. A component that hardcodes a primitive breaks dark mode and breaks rebranding, and a lint rule in Phase 1 enforces this.

### Verified contrast — measured, not claimed

All 26 semantic pairings pass in both modes. Selected values:

| Pairing | Light | Dark | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| text on bg | 19.07:1 | 16.99:1 | 4.5 |
| text-muted on surface | 8.89:1 | 7.35:1 | 4.5 |
| text-subtle on surface | 5.86:1 | 4.69:1 | 4.5 |
| accent on bg | 4.89:1 | 7.65:1 | 4.5 |
| accent-fg on accent | 5.11:1 | 7.24:1 | 4.5 |
| accent on accent-subtle | 4.63:1 | 5.83:1 | 4.5 |
| border-interactive on surface | 3.96:1 | 3.74:1 | 3.0 |
| focus-ring on bg | 4.89:1 | 7.65:1 | 3.0 |
| success on surface | 5.18:1 | 9.17:1 | 4.5 |
| warning on surface | 4.77:1 | 9.78:1 | 4.5 |
| danger on surface | 5.46:1 | 6.97:1 | 4.5 |

Every brand and neutral step is inside the sRGB gamut. Light steps of a saturated hue cannot hold full chroma — `palette.py` binary-searches the maximum in-gamut chroma per lightness and tapers to 98% of it rather than letting the browser clip, which is what produces muddy or neon mid-tones in hand-picked ramps.

**Three border tokens, not two, and the reason matters.** `border-hairline` is decorative (dividers, card edges) and carries no contrast requirement because cards are also distinguished by surface colour. `border-interactive` is used on form controls and must clear 3:1, because when a border is the only thing identifying an input, WCAG 1.4.11 applies to it. Conflating these two is how design systems ship inputs that fail an audit while looking fine.

**The focus ring is a two-layer box-shadow, and the inner layer is load-bearing:**

```css
box-shadow: 0 0 0 2px var(--color-bg), 0 0 0 4px var(--color-focus-ring);
```

A single-layer ring in the accent hue sitting on an accent-filled button is 1.00:1 — mathematically invisible. The bg-coloured spacer guarantees the ring's adjacent colour is always the page background (4.89:1 light, 7.65:1 dark). This is checked in CI.

## 4. Typography

**Faces.** Geist Sans (variable) for display and UI. Geist Mono for metadata, figures, labels, and code. Newsreader for long-form article body on `/insights` only, at 19px/1.7 — a scoped third face is a deliberate editorial signal, and confining it to articles keeps it from becoming decoration.

**Clash Display is retired.** Its replacement is Geist at weight 600 with −0.035em tracking at display sizes. Clash's wide, quirky forms are a strong agency signal, and agency is what is being moved away from; Geist at large sizes with tight negative tracking reads engineered rather than expressive. (Recorded here explicitly because a removal without a named replacement is how build 2 went thin.)

**Weights:** 400 and 600 only, plus 500 for Geist Mono. Two weights far apart read as decided; adjacent weights read as indecision.

**Scale, ratio 1.333.** No freestyle sizes; every size comes from this table.

| Role | px | Tracking | Leading |
|---|---|---|---|
| caption | 12 | +0.01em | 1.4 |
| small | 14 | 0 | 1.5 |
| body | 16 | −0.005em | 1.6 |
| body-lg | 21 | −0.005em | 1.6 |
| h4 | 28 | −0.015em | 1.25 |
| h3 | 37 | −0.025em | 1.15 |
| h2 | 50 | −0.025em | 1.08 |
| h1 | 67 | −0.035em | 1.02 |
| display | 89 | −0.035em | 0.98 |

**Hero display:** `clamp(2.75rem, 6.2vw, 7rem)` — 44px at 375, 112px at 1440. Against 16px body that is a **7:1 ratio**, which clears the ≥6:1 floor in §5. Scale contrast is the single strongest premium signal available, and a page whose largest text is 36px reads as a template regardless of how good the palette is.

**All-caps labels:** +0.08em to +0.12em tracking, never below 11px, Geist Mono 500.

**Measure:** 60–75ch body, 45–60ch for large intro paragraphs, never full-width text at 1440. `text-wrap: balance` on headings, `text-wrap: pretty` on body. Tabular figures on every table, price, stat, and metric.

## 6. Presence budget — what we spend on, named positively

**1. The Capability Graph (the signature graphic).** An SVG diagram of the 18 services as nodes, connected into the four practice areas, laid out radially. Hovering or focusing a node highlights its practice area, dims the rest, and reveals a label; clicking navigates to that service page. It is simultaneously a bespoke graphic, an interactive element, a navigation system, and a content display — which is why it is worth building properly rather than four separate weaker things.

**Geometry, specified so it is not invented:** `viewBox="0 0 960 720"`, centre at 480,340. Four practice-area hubs on a 168px radius from centre, evenly spaced starting at −90°. Service nodes on a 300px radius, clustered within ±32° of their parent hub. Hub nodes are 7px-radius filled circles in `--color-accent`; service nodes are 4.5px circles filled `--color-surface` with a 1.5px `--color-border-interactive` stroke, becoming `--color-accent` fill on hover or focus. Connectors are 1px `--color-border` quadratic curves with the control point pulled 40% toward centre. Labels are Geist Mono 11px, +0.08em tracking, `--color-text-subtle`, becoming `--color-text` on activation. The centre holds the wordmark at 20px. Below 768px the radial layout is replaced by four stacked indexed columns — a radial graph at 375px wide is illegible, and shrinking it is the wrong answer.

**Non-negotiables:** real inline SVG, not canvas, so it is inspectable and accessible. Every node is a real `<a>`. Keyboard-navigable with arrow keys. Server-rendered and fully usable with JavaScript disabled. The SVG is `aria-hidden` with a plain visually-hidden `<ul>` of the same 18 links as the accessible equivalent — an ARIA reimplementation of a graph is worse than a list for every real screen-reader user.

**Honest risk:** this is the one element in the spec that can go wrong aesthetically. A radial node diagram executed loosely looks like a mind-mapping app. What keeps it on the right side is restraint in the resting state — thin 1px connectors, small nodes, most labels hidden until activation — so at rest it reads as a precise technical drawing rather than an infographic. If at Gate 3 it looks like a gimmick, the fallback is a four-column indexed list of all 18 services at large type, and the presence budget shifts to the Scope Estimator plus a second large treated image. Decide that at Gate 3 on the screenshots, not in the abstract.

**2. The Scope Estimator.** Pick service area, scope size, timeline, and support level; get a real price band and an indicative timeline, with a "send me this estimate" lead capture. For a services business this is the highest-value presence element available, because it *is* content — it answers the question every visitor actually has. Must work without JS as a plain multi-step form.

**3. Dramatic type moment.** Hero display at 112px against 16px body, 7:1.

**4. Real imagery, treated.** A single grade applied to all client visuals so mixed sources cohere: consistent warmth, a 1px inner border at `--color-border-hairline` so images do not float, and per-mode treatment (slightly reduced brightness in dark mode via a CSS filter on a wrapper, not baked into the asset).

**5. Genuine content density.** The 18 service pages, the pricing comparison table, the process documentation, and the security/confidentiality page.

**6. Craft at close range.** §9.

## 7. Day / night mode — engineering spec

This is a feature, not a colour swap, and most implementations get it wrong in one of three specific ways.

**Three states, not two.** System / Light / Dark. Defaulting to system and offering no override is a common omission; offering only light/dark and ignoring system is worse. The toggle is a real segmented control with `role="radiogroup"`, not an icon that cycles states with no visible current value.

**No flash, ever.** An inline, blocking script in `<head>` before any paint:

```js
(function(){try{var s=localStorage.getItem('imaginars-theme');var d=s==='dark'||((!s||s==='system')&&matchMedia('(prefers-color-scheme: dark)').matches);document.documentElement.dataset.theme=d?'dark':'light';}catch(e){}})()
```

Because this runs before paint, `[data-theme]` always wins over the `@media (prefers-color-scheme: dark)` block, which exists only as a no-JS fallback. Verification is a screenshot at first paint with a cold cache in both system settings — a theme flash is a Phase 1 gate failure, not a polish item.

**Two `theme-color` meta tags**, one per scheme, using the `media` attribute, so the mobile browser chrome matches. Set `color-scheme` on `:root` in both modes so form controls, scrollbars, and the caret follow.

**Do not animate the swap by transitioning colours.** Transitioning `background-color` across every element repaints the whole page and drops frames. Instead: instant swap by default, with an optional circular reveal via the View Transitions API on toggle click, 350ms `--ease-emphasis`, disabled entirely under `prefers-reduced-motion` and skipped where the API is unsupported.

**Per-mode assets.** Logos, client logos, diagrams, and OG images each need a dark variant. Client logos are the trap — a wordmark supplied as dark-on-transparent disappears on `#07090E`. Every logo gets both variants or a documented plate treatment; a logo rendered on a white rounded plate in dark mode is acceptable and honest, silently inverting a client's brand mark is not.

**Both modes are first-class in verification.** Every axe scan, every screenshot, and every Lighthouse run happens twice. A pass in one mode is not a pass.

## 8b. Grid, breakpoints, header, footer

Specified here because these are where a small site gives itself away, and because leaving them unspecified means the build agent invents them.

**Breakpoints:** 375 / 640 / 768 / 1024 / 1280 / 1440. Design at 1440; verify at 1440, 768, 375.

**Container:** max width 1280px with 24px gutters below 768, 40px to 1280, 64px above. A 680px narrow measure for long-form. A 1440px full-bleed track for imagery only. **Twelve-column grid**, 24px column gap desktop, 16px mobile. Asymmetric splits are 7/5 or 8/4 — a 6/6 split reads as default and is not used for content sections.

**Header.** Sticky, 72px tall, `--color-bg` at 88% opacity with a 12px backdrop blur and a `--color-border-hairline` bottom edge that appears only after 24px of scroll. Contents left to right: wordmark, primary nav, search trigger showing the ⌘K hint, theme control, one primary CTA button.

Primary nav is Services / Work / Pricing / Insights / About, and **Services opens a real mega-menu** — four practice-area columns listing all 18 services with a one-line descriptor each, plus a fifth column holding the current featured case study with its thumbnail. This is not decoration: an 18-service company whose nav hides its services behind a single link looks smaller than it is, and the mega-menu is the single clearest "this is a large company" signal in the chrome. Built on a tested headless menu primitive, opens on hover with a 120ms intent delay and on focus for keyboard, closes on Escape, traps nothing, and is a plain nested `<ul>` when JS is unavailable.

Mobile: full-height drawer, services grouped by practice area in an accordion, theme control and CTA pinned to the bottom.

**Footer.** Five columns plus a base row. Columns: Services by practice area (all 18 linked), Company (about, process, trust, careers, contact), Resources (insights, FAQ, estimator, RSS), Legal (privacy, terms, cookies), and Contact (email, phone, city, response-time commitment). Base row: registered entity name, company registration number, copyright, and the theme control repeated. Publishing the registered entity and a real address is a small detail that separates a company from a freelancer, and it costs nothing.

## 9. Craft details — cheap to implement, disproportionate effect

Layered shadows in light mode, never a single blur. Hairline borders at low opacity rather than heavy greys. Inner top highlight on raised dark surfaces. Designed `:focus-visible` per §3, never `outline: none` without replacement. Hover transitions 120–200ms on everything interactive. `::selection` set to accent. `scroll-margin-top` on every anchor target so deep links clear the sticky header. Custom scrollbar in dark mode. Tabular numerals in all tables and figures. Real typographic characters — em dashes, curly quotes, ×, ½. Skeletons that match final layout dimensions rather than spinners. Designed empty, loading, and error states for the estimator, the search, the contact form, and the filtered case study index. `font-feature-settings` set per face. Icons optically sized — 20px icons need a different stroke weight than 16px.

## 11. Copy rules

Ban: "elevate", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "bespoke solutions", "we don't just X, we Y", "in today's landscape", "unlock", "empower", "robust". No three-item rhetorical triads. No em-dash pivot as a rhythm device. No sentence whose removal changes nothing.

Every claim is falsifiable or it is cut. "Fast turnaround" becomes "first draft in five working days". "Trusted by leading firms" becomes six named logos. Numbers must be real and traceable to a source Soham has supplied.

Voice: plain, specific, declarative. Write like a senior operator explaining scope to a peer, not like a brand.

## 12. Blockers that must be resolved before Phase 2 writes copy

These are unanswered and have been through two builds. The hero currently ships copy an agent invented, which is a large part of why the site does not read as a real company.

1. Who is the buyer? Title, company size, what they were doing ten minutes before landing here.
2. What do they hire Imaginars *instead of* — a freelancer, an agency, an in-house hire, or nothing?
3. What is the one thing true of Imaginars that a competitor cannot copy in a week?
4. What is the actual entry price band, and is publishing it acceptable?
5. Which client names, logos, metrics, and testimonials are cleared for public use, and which need permission first?

**Phase 2 does not write the hero, the value proposition, or any case study claim until these are answered in `content/positioning.md`.** If they are unanswered, Phase 2 builds the templates and leaves the copy slots marked `TODO(positioning)` — visibly, in the build — rather than inventing filler.

## 13. Self-audit before delivery

1. Which register, and can it be defended against the other three? (§2)
2. Type ratio ≥ 6:1, measured in-browser?
3. Name the one element that makes this look designed rather than assembled. If it cannot be named, it is not there.
4. Exactly one accent hue and one radius language?
5. Which presence elements were spent on, named?
6. Anywhere something was removed without its replacement being added?
7. Would this be identifiable as this brand with the logo removed?
8. Empty, loading, error, focus states designed — in both modes?
9. Section padding ≥ 128px desktop?
10. Holds at 1440, 768, 375, and at 200% zoom, in both modes?
11. Every content floor item C1–C8 met?
12. Does any component reference a tier-1 primitive directly?
13. Does the theme survive a cold load with no flash, in both system settings?
14. Is there exactly one motion signature?
15. Does the navigation expose the full range of capability, or does it hide 18 services behind one link?
16. Would a buyer reading one service page end to end learn something they could act on?
